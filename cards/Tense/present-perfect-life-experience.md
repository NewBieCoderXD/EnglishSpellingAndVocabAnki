# Front

<div class="hint">Present perfect vs. simple past &middot; life experience, no time marker</div>

Type the verb form:

<span class="ex">"I __________ Japan three times."</span>

---

# Type

have visited

---

# Back

I **<span class="ans">have visited</span>** Japan three times.

It treats your trips as part of your <b>life experience up to now</b> — they connect your past to the person you are today. The present perfect does not say <i>when</i>; it says "as of now, this is true of me."

With the simple past, <span class="ex">"I visited Japan three times"</span> sounds like a <b>closed chapter</b>, usually tied to a specific period that is over.

<div class="pat">experience up to now &rarr; present perfect &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; specific finished trip &rarr; simple past</div>

<span class="no">visited</span> / <span class="no">have visit</span> — the perfect takes the past participle.

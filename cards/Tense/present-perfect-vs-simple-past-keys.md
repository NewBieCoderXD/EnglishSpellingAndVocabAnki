# Front

<div class="hint">Present perfect vs. simple past &middot; the result still matters now</div>

Type the verb form:

<span class="ex">"I __________ my keys — and I still can't find them."</span>

---

# Type

have lost

---

# Back

I **<span class="ans">have lost</span>** my keys — and I still can't find them.

- <b>I have lost my keys</b> <span class="pos">present perfect</span> — connects the past to the present: the loss <b>still matters now</b>. The natural reading is that you still do not have them.
- <b>I lost my keys</b> <span class="pos">simple past</span> — a past event, reported as finished. It says nothing about now; you may well have found them again since.

<div class="pat">just the event &rarr; simple past &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; event with a present result &rarr; present perfect</div>

The second half of the sentence picks the tense for you: a <b>present result</b> ("still can't find them") forces the present perfect.
